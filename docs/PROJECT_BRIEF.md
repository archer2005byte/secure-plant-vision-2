# Secure Power Vision

Project intent

Create a premium B2B pitch microsite for EY titled Integrated Surveillance and

Security Modernization for Power Generation Assets.

Audience: power generation companies in India, including PSU generators, state

generation companies, and high-risk border-area plants.

Tone: corporate, consulting-grade, crisp, credible, modern, not flashy.

Visual style: deep blue, white, grey, subtle accent color, premium cards, strong

spacing, icon-led layouts, clean typography, responsive design.

Goal: position EY as a strategic advisor for surveillance, security modernization,

command-centre design, DPR/RFP support, vendor onboarding, and implementation

governance for power generation companies.

Section 1: Hero

Build a strong hero section with a clear title, one-line value proposition, and a call-to-

action.

Title:

Integrated Surveillance and Security Modernization for Power Generation

Assets

Subtitle:

Helping power generation companies strengthen perimeter security, plant

surveillance, operational visibility, and incident response through a structured

advisory-led approach.

CTA buttons:

 Explore the framework

 Request an assessment

Visual direction:

Use a professional industrial/security visual with subtle command-centre cues, not a

stock-photo-heavy layout.

Section 2: Why this matters now

Create a section explaining why security modernization is now a priority for power

generation companies.

Content direction:

 Power assets are critical infrastructure.

 Risks now include perimeter intrusion, theft, insider movement, unsafe

access, sabotage, contractor control gaps, and continuity threats.

 Border-area and sensitive plants have additional geopolitical and law-and-

order exposure.

 Many plants have partial surveillance, but not an integrated security

architecture.

 Security is no longer just CCTV; it is a plant-wide operational risk function.

Design:

Use 3 to 5 crisp insight cards with icons.

Section 3: Power generation company segments

Create a section that shows three distinct customer segments and how their needs

differ.

Segment 1:

PSU generation companies

 Existing surveillance base

 Need enhancement, integration, analytics, and better governance

Segment 2:

State generation companies

 Lower surveillance maturity

 Need baseline modernisation, prioritisation, and phased rollout

Segment 3:

Border / sensitive-area power plants

 Higher threat exposure

 Need layered security, resilience, perimeter intelligence, and faster response

Design:

Present as three side-by-side cards with a short “what they need” line under each.

Section 4: Major blocks of a power generation plant

Create a section that shows the major physical and operational blocks of a typical

power generation company.

Use a grid or zonal map with these blocks:

 Plant boundary and perimeter

 Entry and access-control zone

 Fuel receipt and storage area

 Fuel handling system

 Power-generation block

 Electrical systems and evacuation infrastructure

 Control and command facilities

 Water systems

 Waste and environmental systems

 Stores and logistics

 Administrative and personnel areas

 Residential and township areas

 Emergency and safety infrastructure

 Digital and communication infrastructure

 Remote and off-site assets

For each block, include one short line on why it matters from a surveillance and

security perspective.

Design:

Use a clean industrial zoning layout, not a generic checklist.

Section 5: As-is assessment

Create a section titled As-Is Security Assessment.

Explain that EY would assess the current security posture across the plant and its

associated assets.

Cover the following dimensions:

 Site security architecture

 CCTV inventory and health

 Camera placement adequacy

 Day/night visibility

 Perimeter protection coverage

 Gate and access-control controls

 Visitor and contractor movement

 Vehicle movement and material movement

 Security manpower deployment

 Command centre or control room capability

 Incident logging and evidence management

 Network, storage, and retention

 Integration with fire, safety, and operational systems where relevant

 SOPs, escalation, and drills

 AMC, uptime, spares, and SLA readiness

 Compliance and procurement gaps

Close this section with a line saying the assessment creates the fact base for the to-

be design, costing, and procurement plan.

Design:

Use a two-column layout with “What we examine” and “What it reveals.”

Section 6: To-be security architecture

Create a section titled To-Be Integrated Security Architecture.

Show the target model as a flow:

Field devices and sensors → network and edge layer → video management

and analytics → command centre → incident response and management

dashboards

Include components such as:

 CCTV

 Thermal cameras

 PTZ cameras

 Access control

 Visitor management

 ANPR

 Perimeter sensors

 Alarm integration

 Command centre dashboard

 Incident workflow

 Evidence archive

 Response escalation

Include a short note that the to-be design should be tailored to plant type, threat

profile, and current infrastructure maturity.

Design:

Use a layered architecture diagram or a horizontal process flow.

Section 7: Key surveillance and security use cases

Create a section showing practical use cases, not abstract technology language.

Include these use cases:

 Perimeter intrusion

 Loitering

 Unauthorised entry

 Restricted-zone access

 Vehicle movement at wrong gate or zone

 Contractor non-compliance

 Object left behind

 Camera tampering

 Smoke or fire visual alert

 Crowd or labour congregation

 Drone or low-flying object detection where relevant

For each use case, show:

 what it detects

 why it matters

 how the control room responds

Design:

Use cards or tabs with a short response workflow under each.

Section 8: EY service offerings

Create a section titled How EY Can Support.

Include the following offerings:

 Security maturity assessment

 Site risk assessment

 Gap assessment

 To-be architecture

 DPR preparation

 RFP and technical specification drafting

 BoQ and cost estimation support

 Vendor evaluation support

 Implementation PMU or PMC

 FAT, SAT, and UAT support

 SLA and O&M framework

 SOP design

 Training and change management

Position EY as vendor-neutral, governance-led, and implementation-oriented.

Design:

Use a service matrix or capability cards.

Section 9: Delivery roadmap

Create a section titled Engagement Roadmap.

Show a phased journey:

1. Assessment and risk mapping

2. Concept design and priorities

3. DPR / RFP preparation

4. Vendor onboarding and selection

5. Implementation governance

6. Testing, training, and go-live

7. O&M monitoring and continuous improvement

For each phase, include one line on the output of that phase.

Design:

Use a stepper or timeline.

Section 10: Why EY

Create a final credibility section on why EY is suited for this work.

Include these points:

 Independent advisory

 Security architecture and procurement discipline

 Experience bridging operations, technology, governance, and risk

 Ability to design for heterogeneous plant environments

 Structured support from assessment to implementation

Keep the language restrained and credible. Avoid vendor-like language.

Design:

Use a premium closing panel with a strong statement and a CTA.

Section 11: Closing CTA

Create a final call-to-action section.

Suggested copy:

Start with a structured security assessment for one pilot plant or one

operating cluster. EY can help define the current state, target state,

implementation roadmap, and procurement-ready roadmap.

CTA buttons:

 Book an assessment discussion

 View the delivery framework

Design:

Simple, strong, boardroom-ready.

Build instructions for Lovable

 Make the site feel like a consulting pitch, not a marketing brochure.

 Use concise copy.

 Use icons, diagrams, and section cards instead of long paragraphs.

 Keep the entire flow highly structured and easy to present in a client meeting.

 Make it responsive.

 Ensure every section can stand alone in a sales discussion.

 Use placeholders where detailed client-specific facts are not yet known.
